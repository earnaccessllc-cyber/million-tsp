G fund vs C fund

Safety versus growth in the TSP, and how to decide how much of each you need.

Updated October 27, 2026 · MillionFed Guides

The G Fund and the C Fund sit at opposite ends of the TSP. One can never lose principal; the other owns the 500 biggest U.S. companies and moves with the stock market. Most federal employees end up holding some of both, in proportions that shift as retirement gets closer.

Side by side

G FundC Fund
HoldsSpecial-issue U.S. Treasury securitiesS&P 500 stocks
Can it lose money?No, principal is guaranteedYes: about −37% in 2008 and −18% in 2022

The case for the G Fund

The G Fund never has a losing year. If you are a few years from retiring, or you will need cash soon, the G Fund protects that money from a market crash. It is also useful as a buffer: some retirees keep a few years of withdrawals in G so they never have to sell stocks during a downturn.

The case for the C Fund

Over decades, stocks have rewarded patience. A 30-year-old who keeps everything in the G Fund is very likely to retire with far less than one invested mostly in stocks, because G returns have often barely kept up with inflation. The C Fund's drops have historically been temporary for investors who kept holding and kept contributing.

The biggest mistake: switching after a crash

Moving everything from C to G after a big drop turns a temporary loss into a permanent one, and investors who do this often miss the recovery. If a drop would make you sell, that is a sign to hold more G before the next one, not after.

A simple way to think about the split

  • Money needed within about 5 years: G (and F) fund territory.
  • Money you will not touch for 10+ years: stock funds like C, often alongside S and I.
  • Want it automatic: an L Fund holds both and moves from C toward G for you over time.
