FERS COLA in 2027: Who Gets the 2.6% Increase?

The federal retirement cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for payments beginning in January 2027 is now known. But a headline percentage does not mean every FERS retiree gets a raise. Your age, retirement category, annuity start date and whether you receive the FERS supplement all matter.

Quick answer: OPM lists a 2.6% FERS COLA for 2027 for annuitants entitled to the full increase, compared with 3.6% for CSRS. The increase is effective December 1, 2026, and normally appears in the January 2027 payment. Most regular FERS retirees under age 62 receive no FERS basic-annuity COLA, even if they retired with an unreduced pension. Special-category, disability and survivor exceptions exist. See OPM's current COLA FAQ and OPM's payment timing FAQ.

How much is the 2027 FERS COLA?

OPM reports a 2.6% cost-of-living adjustment for eligible FERS annuitants in 2027. The corresponding CSRS increase is 3.6%. These are not estimates for future years: they are the published adjustments effective December 1, 2026, reflected in the benefit paid in January 2027. Your actual change can be smaller if the initial increase is prorated or if other individual rules apply. OPM applies the percentage to the eligible gross annuity and rounds the new monthly amount down to a whole dollar. OPM: how COLAs are calculated.

When do regular FERS retirees start receiving COLAs?

Under the usual FERS rules, cost-of-living increases to the basic pension begin at age 62. That is separate from retirement eligibility. For example, someone born in 1970 or later can generally reach their minimum retirement age (MRA) at 57 and may qualify for an immediate unreduced pension with 30 years of service. But being entitled to that pension at 57 does not itself entitle them to annual FERS COLAs at 57, 58, 59, 60 or 61. OPM: FERS computation and COLA eligibility.

For a regular FERS employee who retires at 57, that can mean several years of unchanged nominal basic pension while living costs rise. There is no automatic retroactive catch-up payment for all the COLAs missed before age 62. Once eligible, the annuity follows the COLA rules then in effect; the 2027 percentage should not be assumed for a future year. This timing matters when building a retirement spending plan.

Does the first COLA at age 62 get prorated?

Not necessarily. OPM generally prorates a first COLA for an eligible annuitant who has been on the annuity rolls for less than a full year: one-twelfth of the increase for each eligible month. But OPM's CSRS/FERS Handbook specifically says that FERS annuitants who were ineligible for COLAs during their first year or more on the annuity rolls receive the full first COLA once they become eligible, without that first-year proration. A regular FERS retiree who started an annuity at 57 and becomes COLA-eligible at 62 is an important example of why the ordinary new-retiree proration rule cannot be applied blindly. The date you reach 62 relative to the December 1 adjustment date also matters. OPM CSRS/FERS Handbook, Chapter 40, section 40A2.1-3(O).

Who can receive a FERS COLA before age 62?

OPM lists exceptions to the usual age-62 rule, including certain FERS disability annuitants, survivor annuitants and people retired under qualifying special provisions for law enforcement officers, firefighters and air traffic controllers. Some military reserve technicians also have special rules. These are statutory retirement categories, not simply hazardous or physically demanding job titles. OPM: eligibility exceptions.

For disability retirement, OPM says a FERS disability annuity based on 60% of high-3 pay generally does not receive a COLA during the first 12 months; subsequent adjustments can apply under the disability rules. A FERS annuity with a CSRS component may have different COLA treatment for that component. Ask OPM or your agency to review mixed-service and disability cases rather than assuming the regular age-62 rule always controls. OPM: FERS retirement types and disability computation.

How is the FERS COLA calculated?

OPM uses the third-quarter average change in the CPI-W (Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers), calculated by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. FERS has a different formula from the CSRS increase when inflation exceeds 2%:

CPI-W increaseFERS COLA formula
2% or lessSame percentage as the CPI-W increase
More than 2% but not more than 3%2%
More than 3%CPI-W increase minus 1 percentage point

For the 2027 adjustment, the 3.6% CPI-W-based increase translates to a 2.6% FERS COLA. The formula explains why the FERS percentage can be lower than the CSRS percentage. Do not confuse the CPI-W figure, the Social Security COLA, and the FERS pension COLA; eligibility and program rules still determine what a particular retiree receives. OPM: COLA determination.

Example: a $2,000 monthly FERS pension in January 2027

Suppose two retirees each have a $2,000 gross monthly FERS basic annuity before the adjustment. One is eligible for the full 2027 COLA; the other is a regular FERS retiree age 59 without a special exception. Holding all other factors constant:

RetireeIllustrative gross monthly pension
Eligible for full 2.6% COLA$2,000 × 1.026 = $2,052
Age 59, regular FERS, no exception$2,000 (no COLA)

This is an illustration of the gross basic annuity, not a guaranteed deposit. Survivor elections, taxes, insurance premiums, proration, annuity limits and other deductions may change the net payment. It also does not imply that every age-62 retiree automatically qualifies for a full COLA in the same December cycle.

Does the FERS Special Retirement Supplement get a COLA?

No. OPM explicitly states that the FERS retiree annuity supplement—often called the SRS—is not increased by COLAs. It is a separate temporary benefit from the basic pension. Eligible regular FERS retirees may receive it before age 62, subject to its own rules and possible earnings-test reduction, but a 2.6% basic-annuity COLA does not raise the supplement. See OPM's FERS election and supplement guidance and MillionFed's supplement earnings-test explainer.

USPS letter carriers: does a postal retirement change the COLA rule?

A regular FERS-covered USPS city letter carrier generally follows the same basic age-62 COLA rule as other regular FERS employees. Working a physically demanding postal route does not by itself make the position a special-category law enforcement or firefighter retirement. A carrier who retires at MRA 57 with 30 qualifying years may receive an immediate pension and, if otherwise eligible, the FERS supplement, but normally will not receive a basic-annuity COLA until the applicable age-62 rules are met. Confirm the actual retirement provision, service record and annuity computation with USPS retirement personnel and OPM.

The Postal Service Health Benefits (PSHB) program, TSP investments and Social Security have separate rules. A FERS pension COLA does not automatically increase your TSP account balance, and changes in health insurance premiums can affect take-home retirement income even when the gross annuity increases.

Checklist for planning around FERS COLAs

  1. Verify whether your retirement is regular FERS, special-category, disability, survivor or mixed CSRS/FERS.
  2. Write down your 62nd birthday and compare it with the December 1 annual COLA effective date.
  3. Request an official gross basic-annuity estimate from your agency, including any survivor election.
  4. Model several years without pension COLAs if you plan to retire before 62 under regular FERS.
  5. Keep the FERS supplement separate in your budget; it has no COLA and ends at 62.
  6. Check OPM's current annual COLA notice rather than assuming 2027's 2.6% will repeat.
