When Can Federal Employees Retire Under FERS?

For most federal employees covered by the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS), retirement eligibility depends on both age and creditable service. Reaching your minimum retirement age (MRA) alone does not necessarily qualify you for an unreduced pension.

Quick answer: Under the standard FERS rules, an immediate, unreduced retirement generally requires your MRA with 30 years of service, age 60 with 20 years, or age 62 with 5 years. An MRA + 10 retirement can be available earlier with a reduced annuity. Special-category employees and other retirement provisions have different rules.

These are the general rules for regular FERS employees. Disability, early-out, discontinued-service, and certain special-category retirements have additional provisions. See OPM's official FERS retirement types.

What is the FERS minimum retirement age (MRA)?

Your MRA is based on your birth year. Employees born in 1970 or later have an MRA of 57. Those born from 1948 through 1969 generally have an MRA between 55 and 56 years, 10 months, depending on birth year. Check OPM's MRA chart for your exact birth year.

What happens if you retire at 57?

If your MRA is 57 and you have 30 years of qualifying service, you may qualify for an immediate, unreduced FERS basic annuity. If you have 25 years instead, reaching age 57 does not make the pension unreduced under the ordinary MRA + 30 rule. You may instead qualify for MRA + 10, with a potential age reduction or a postponed start date.

How much is the basic FERS pension?

For a regular FERS retirement, the basic annual annuity generally uses your high-3 average basic salary × creditable service × 1%. The multiplier generally increases to 1.1% if you retire at age 62 or older with at least 20 years of service. These are gross estimates before applicable reductions and deductions. Overtime and bonuses generally are not included in high-3 basic pay. Source: OPM computation rules.

Example: retire at 57 with 30 years

Assume a $90,000 high-3 salary and 30 years of creditable service. The standard formula gives $90,000 × 30 × 1% = $27,000 a year, or $2,250 a month before deductions or reductions. This is an illustration, not an individual benefit estimate.

Example: retire at 62 with 30 years

With the same $90,000 high-3, the 1.1% formula gives $90,000 × 30 × 1.1% = $29,700 a year, or $2,475 a month before deductions or reductions. This comparison isolates the multiplier; real high-3 pay and service may differ.

Does unused sick leave help you qualify?

Unused sick leave may increase service credited in your pension calculation once you are otherwise eligible, but it generally cannot be used to meet the minimum service requirement for retirement eligibility. OPM explains this distinction.

Can you receive the FERS supplement before 62?

Some employees retiring with an immediate, unreduced FERS annuity—such as at MRA with 30 years or age 60 with 20—may qualify for the FERS annuity supplement before age 62. It is not the same as Social Security, and an earnings test can apply. MRA + 10 retirees generally do not receive this supplement. Eligibility varies by retirement provision; verify with your agency.

Retirement planning checklist

  1. Confirm your birth-year MRA and your agency's record of creditable service.
  2. Identify whether you qualify under MRA + 30, 60 + 20, 62 + 5, or another provision.
  3. Request your official high-3 and pension estimate from your agency.
  4. Review FEHB continuation, survivor elections, taxes, and possible pension reductions.
  5. Plan how your TSP withdrawals and any FERS supplement fit into your retirement income.
