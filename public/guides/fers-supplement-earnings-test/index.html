FERS Supplement Earnings Test: How Much Can You Earn in 2026 and 2027?

Retiring from federal service does not always mean stopping work. But if you qualify for the FERS Special Retirement Supplement (also called the FERS annuity supplement or SRS), wages from a second job or net earnings from self-employment can reduce that supplement. The rules are different from the rules for your basic FERS pension.

Quick answer: The annual earnings-test limit is $24,480 for 2026 and $25,560 for 2027. When the FERS supplement earnings test applies, OPM generally reduces the supplement by $1 for every $2 of countable earnings above the applicable annual limit. Your basic FERS annuity is not reduced by this particular test. The limits are based on the Social Security Administration's lower annual exempt amount, not its higher full-retirement-age-year threshold. OPM explanation; SSA limits.

What is the FERS supplement?

The FERS annuity supplement is a temporary payment intended to approximate the Social Security benefit attributable to covered federal service, before age 62. It is separate from the FERS basic annuity, your Thrift Savings Plan (TSP), and any Social Security benefit you later claim. It stops at age 62, even if you delay Social Security. It is not automatically equal to your age-62 Social Security estimate. OPM: FERS supplement fact sheet.

SSA publishes these exempt amounts annually. Its separate, higher limit for the year someone reaches Social Security full retirement age is not the figure to use for a FERS supplement that ends at age 62. OPM's survey page still displays the 2025 figure, so check the SSA annual table for newer years and follow the instructions on your OPM survey. The amount applicable to your case depends on the calendar year being reported.

How does the $1-for-$2 reduction work?

For a straightforward full-year illustration using the 2026 limit, assume a retiree is receiving a $1,500 monthly FERS supplement and has $34,480 of countable post-retirement wages in 2026:

  1. Countable earnings: $34,480.
  2. Subtract the 2026 exempt amount: $34,480 − $24,480 = $10,000 over the limit.
  3. Divide the excess by two: $10,000 ÷ 2 = $5,000 potential annual supplement reduction.
  4. Illustrative annual supplement before the test: $1,500 × 12 = $18,000; after the $5,000 reduction, $13,000.

This is a simplified annual example, not a prediction of when OPM will adjust monthly payments. OPM's survey and payment timing matter; reductions cannot exceed the supplement otherwise payable. The basic FERS pension is unaffected by this earnings test. OPM survey FAQ.

What income counts against the supplement?

OPM generally looks at earned income after retirement, including wages and net earnings from self-employment. Examples of potentially countable income include:

  • W-2 wages from a post-retirement job, including overtime, bonuses, vacation pay and certain severance payments.
  • Net earnings from operating or managing your own business, after allowable business expenses.
  • Certain deferred compensation for post-retirement work and gross federal reemployment earnings before an annuity offset.

OPM instructs retirees not to include earnings from before retirement in the survey's post-retirement earned-income figure. Timing and characterization can be nuanced, especially for deferred pay and business income; follow the actual survey instructions. OPM: what to report.

What income generally does not count?

According to OPM's survey guidance, the following generally are not earned income for this test:

  • Your FERS basic pension or other retirement annuities.
  • TSP and 401(k) withdrawals.
  • Interest, dividends, and capital gains from investments.
  • Social Security benefits and veterans benefits.
  • Ordinary rental income or royalties unless received in the course of a trade or business.

Important for side businesses: Do not assume that income is excluded simply because you receive a Form 1099 or have an LLC. Net earnings from self-employment can count. Passive rental income and actively operating a rental or other business may be treated differently depending on the facts. Ask OPM or a qualified tax professional about uncertain classifications. Who qualifies for the FERS supplement?

Under ordinary FERS provisions, employees who retire with an immediate, unreduced annuity at their minimum retirement age (MRA) with 30 years of service, or at age 60 with 20 years, can generally qualify. Certain approved early-out and discontinued-service retirees may qualify, but the supplement does not start until MRA. People who retire under MRA + 10 or FERS disability retirement are generally not eligible. OPM eligibility guidance.

Special-category rules differ. Certain law enforcement officers, firefighters, air traffic controllers and military reserve technicians may receive the supplement before their regular MRA. OPM states that, for qualifying special-category retirees, the supplement's earnings test generally does not apply until they reach MRA. This exception is not a blanket rule for every federal worker. OPM special-category example.

Does this apply to USPS letter carriers?

USPS employees covered by FERS generally follow the regular FERS age-and-service rules, unless they have a separate qualifying retirement provision. A city letter carrier retiring at MRA with at least 30 years of creditable service may qualify for the supplement; that job title alone does not grant the special-category pre-MRA earnings-test exception. Verify service and retirement eligibility through USPS retirement benefits personnel and OPM. For a refresher, see MillionFed's FERS retirement eligibility guide.

Do you have to complete the OPM supplement survey?

OPM uses a FERS Annuity Supplement Survey to ask about prior-year earnings after retirement. OPM says retirees with no earnings, or earnings below the applicable limit, generally do not need to return the form unless their supplement was reduced in a previous year. Do not ignore a notice directed to you: read the instructions and respond when required. The OPM page currently discusses the 2025 earnings year, so verify which year your particular form covers. OPM survey FAQ.

Five questions to check before taking a retirement job

  1. Will your retirement provision actually qualify you for the FERS supplement?
  2. Have you reached MRA, and does a special-category exception apply?
  3. How much post-retirement earned income—not total cash flow—do you expect this calendar year?
  4. Could your side business generate countable self-employment earnings?
  5. Can your retirement budget handle a reduced or zero supplement until age 62?
