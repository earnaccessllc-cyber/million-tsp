TSP Beneficiary Designations: Who Inherits Your Account?

Who actually gets your TSP if you die? This MillionFed guide explains the beneficiary designation, the federal default order, and why your will or FERS survivor election may not control the account.

Quick answer: A valid TSP beneficiary designation generally controls who receives the account. Without one, the federal statutory order begins with a surviving spouse, then children, parents, estate representatives, and next of kin.

Why your TSP beneficiary designation matters

A Thrift Savings Plan account does not necessarily pass to the person named in your will. The TSP generally follows the valid beneficiary designation on file at the time of death; when there is none, federal law supplies an order of precedence. That can produce a different result from what family members expect. The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) explains this distinction in its TSP retirement guidance.

Who inherits a TSP if you did not name a beneficiary?

When no valid designation is on file, the TSP follows this statutory order: (1) surviving spouse; (2) children equally, with a deceased child's share passing to that child's descendants by representation; (3) parents equally or the surviving parent; (4) the duly appointed executor or administrator of the estate; and (5) next of kin entitled under the law of the state where the participant lived at death. The TSP does not simply divide the balance among everyone in those categories: it pays the first eligible category. See OPM's TSP-specific order of precedence.

A stepchild is not always treated as a child under the default order

Under the default order, the term child includes a biological or adopted child but not a stepchild who was not adopted. Similarly, a stepparent who did not adopt the participant is not a parent for this purpose. If your intended recipient is a stepchild, partner, relative, charity, or trust, do not assume the default order will accomplish that goal. Check the actual designation accepted by the TSP. The official TSP precedence explanation describes these distinctions.

Does your will override a TSP beneficiary?

Generally, no. In OPM's published FAQ, the Federal Retirement Thrift Investment Board explains that a will does not override a valid TSP designation. The TSP designation is therefore a separate part of estate planning. Court orders and unusual disputes can raise additional issues, so obtain qualified legal guidance when those are involved. Read OPM's beneficiary FAQ with FRTIB responses.

How do you check or change your TSP beneficiary?

Use the official TSP website and sign in to your account to review the beneficiary information the plan has on record. OPM's survivor-benefits guidance directs participants to their TSP account to review and update TSP beneficiaries. Follow the current instructions in the account rather than relying on old mailing addresses or an outdated copy of a TSP-3 form found elsewhere online. Confirm that the designation was successfully recorded; saving a draft or telling a relative your wishes is not the same as having a valid designation on file.

When should federal employees review their designations?

Marriage, divorce, a new child, adoption, a beneficiary's death, retirement, or a major change in family circumstances are sensible triggers for a review. For example, someone who named a former partner years ago should not assume a later marriage automatically replaced that named person. Conversely, someone without a designation should check whether the statutory order reflects their wishes. Review the actual TSP record after each change rather than guessing what it says.

USPS letter carriers: TSP, FERS pension and life insurance are separate

The TSP beneficiary designation governs TSP death benefits. It is not the same as a FERS spouse survivor annuity election, a designation for any FERS lump-sum benefit, or a Federal Employees' Group Life Insurance (FEGLI) beneficiary designation. OPM says different forms and records apply to these benefits. This is true for USPS employees covered by FERS as well as other civilian federal employees. For the pension decision, read MillionFed's FERS survivor benefit options guide. OPM explains the separate designations.

Does divorce automatically change the TSP beneficiary?

Do not assume it does. A divorce settlement, an accepted TSP retirement benefits court order, and the participant's beneficiary designation address different rights. The TSP Court Order Center reviews qualifying orders, and a court order may impose separate obligations. If divorce is involved, read MillionFed's TSP divorce and court-order guide, verify the beneficiary designation on file, and obtain legal advice on any order or settlement terms. The official TSP Court Order Center explains its role.

What happens after a TSP participant dies?

The TSP operates a Life Events Hub for reporting a participant's death, uploading documents, and tracking a claim. The portal describes identity verification and document requirements. Beneficiaries should use the official process and follow the TSP's instructions rather than sending sensitive information to an unofficial service. Payment timing, available options, and taxes depend on the circumstances; this guide does not promise a distribution date or a particular tax result.

A five-minute beneficiary review checklist

Sign in directly at tsp.gov; check whether a valid designation is recorded; verify names and intended shares; consider whether family changes have made it outdated; separately review FERS and FEGLI elections; and retain confirmation of any successfully completed change. If a court order or trust is involved, consult the plan and qualified counsel before assuming the designation achieves your intended result.

Frequently asked questions

Does my spouse automatically inherit my TSP?

If there is no valid designation, a surviving spouse is first in the statutory order. But a valid designation can direct payment differently, subject to applicable law and any relevant orders.

Can I name a trust?

FRTIB's answer published by OPM says a TSP participant may designate a trust. Review the TSP's current requirements and seek qualified estate-planning advice about the trust language.

Is my FERS survivor annuity beneficiary the same as my TSP beneficiary?

No. A FERS survivor annuity election and a TSP beneficiary designation govern different benefits.

Where do survivors report a death?

Use the official TSP Life Events Hub at lifeevents.tsp.gov and follow its verification and document instructions.
