How to calculate your FERS pension

The formula, the 1.1% bonus, your MRA by birth year and real examples, without the jargon.

Updated October 27, 2026 · MillionFed Guides

Your FERS pension, called the basic annuity, is one of three parts of federal retirement, alongside Social Security and your TSP. The formula is simple once you know three numbers: your high-3 salary, your years of service, and your age when you retire.

The FERS pension formula

Annual pension = high-3 salary × years of service × 1%
The multiplier rises to 1.1% if you retire at age 62 or older with at least 20 years of service.

What counts as your high-3

Your high-3 is the average of your highest 36 consecutive months of basic pay, usually your last three years. Basic pay includes locality pay but not overtime, bonuses or most allowances.

What counts as service

Creditable service includes your years and months of federal civilian work, military service you have made a deposit for, and unused sick leave, which is added to your service when your pension is calculated.

The FERS supplement

If you retire on an immediate, unreduced pension before 62 (for example at your MRA with 30 years, or at 60 with 20), you may also receive the FERS special retirement supplement. It approximates the Social Security benefit earned during your FERS service and is paid until 62, when you become eligible for Social Security. It is subject to an earnings limit if you keep working, and it is not paid to people who retire under MRA + 10.

Cost-of-living adjustments

Regular FERS retirees generally begin receiving COLAs at age 62. When inflation is above 2%, the FERS COLA is smaller than the Social Security COLA under what is often called the "diet COLA" rule.

For your official numbers, request a retirement estimate from your agency's HR office or check your agency's retirement calculator. OPM makes the final calculation when you retire.
