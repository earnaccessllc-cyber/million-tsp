FERS Postponed vs. Deferred Retirement: The Differences That Matter

Leaving federal service before your pension starts can mean two very different things: a postponed MRA+10 retirement or a deferred FERS retirement. They can have similar pension start dates but very different consequences for health insurance, life insurance and survivor benefits. This guide explains how to tell them apart.

Short answer: If you separate at or after your minimum retirement age (MRA) with at least 10 years of qualifying service, you may be eligible for an MRA+10 retirement and choose to postpone the pension start. If you leave before you qualify for an immediate retirement, your eventual pension is generally deferred. Under OPM rules, an eligible postponed MRA+10 retiree may be able to resume federal health and life insurance when payments begin; a deferred retiree cannot reenroll in those benefits through the deferred annuity. What counts as an MRA+10 retirement?

For most regular FERS employees, MRA+10 means you have reached your minimum retirement age and completed at least 10 years of creditable service, but you do not qualify for an immediate unreduced annuity under MRA+30, age 60+20 or age 62+5. The MRA depends on your birth year: it is 57 for people born in 1970 or later, and lower for some earlier birth years. Special-category retirement and involuntary early-out provisions follow different rules. OPM: FERS eligibility and MRA chart.

Starting an MRA+10 annuity before age 62 usually causes a permanent reduction of 5/12 of 1% for each full month before age 62 (5% for each full year). Postponing the start reduces the penalty. With at least 20 years of service, starting at age 60 or later removes the MRA+10 age reduction. This does not create extra years of service or increase the high-3 salary you had at separation. OPM: FERS computation.

Three examples: the same departure date can change your options

Example 1: Leave at 57 with 15 years

Assume your MRA is 57 and your unreduced annual FERS annuity formula produces $15,000. If you start an MRA+10 pension immediately at 57, you are five years under 62: a simplified 25% age reduction gives $11,250 a year before other deductions or elections. If you instead postpone to 62, the age reduction is eliminated, but you receive no annuity during the waiting years. These numbers are illustrations, not an OPM estimate.

Example 2: Leave at 57 with 20 years

You have MRA+10 eligibility at separation. If you postpone pension payments until age 60, the age reduction is eliminated under the 20-year exception. But this is still an MRA+10 retirement: postponement does not turn it into a regular age-60 retirement for purposes of the FERS annuity supplement. OPM: FERS supplement example.

Example 3: Leave at 52 with 20 years

Assuming no special early-retirement provision applies, you are below MRA at separation, so this is not a postponed MRA+10 retirement. A deferred annuity may be available later; OPM says the age reduction can be eliminated if you have 20 years and begin at 60. The deferred annuity does not restore federal health or life insurance. OPM: deferred retirement.

Health insurance is the biggest practical difference

For a qualifying postponed MRA+10 retirement, OPM explains that FEHB coverage generally stops when you separate and can resume when your postponed annuity begins if you met the continuation requirements at separation. The familiar rule is generally coverage for the five years of service immediately before retirement, or since the first opportunity to enroll if shorter, subject to detailed exceptions. Do not assume that merely having 10 years of federal service satisfies the insurance rule. OPM: health coverage continuation.

During the gap, eligible separating employees may elect Temporary Continuation of Coverage (TCC) for up to 18 months. OPM states that TCC generally costs the full employee-plus-government premium plus 2%; contact the former agency promptly about election deadlines. That gap may last longer than TCC coverage if you postpone for several years. OPM: postponed retirement insurance options.

For a deferred annuity, OPM explicitly says former employees cannot reenroll in FEHB, FEGLI or federal dental/vision coverage through that deferred retirement. This is why leaving before MRA can be materially different from separating under MRA+10—even when both pensions start at age 60 or 62. OPM: deferred health and life coverage.

USPS employees: check PSHB, not just FEHB

Since January 1, 2025, eligible Postal Service employees and annuitants generally use the Postal Service Health Benefits (PSHB) program rather than regular FEHB plans. OPM confirms that qualifying USPS employees who lose coverage at separation may be eligible for up to 18 months of PSHB TCC, also at the full premium plus 2%. Ask USPS benefits personnel and OPM to confirm your specific PSHB continuation and reenrollment eligibility before selecting a postponed date; general FEHB explanations do not by themselves settle an individual postal case. OPM: PSHB program.

What about federal life insurance (FEGLI)?

With a postponed MRA+10 annuity, FEGLI enrollment generally stops at separation; eligible prior coverage can be reinstated when the postponed annuity begins if the continuation requirements were met. With a deferred annuity, you do not regain FEGLI as a retiree. This is separate from buying an individual policy during the gap. OPM: insurance options.

Do postponed or deferred retirees get the FERS Special Retirement Supplement?

No, not under these retirement categories. OPM states that the FERS annuity supplement is not payable under the MRA+10 or deferred retirement provisions. Starting an MRA+10 annuity at age 60 without an age reduction does not change the original retirement provision. The supplement has its own eligibility rules for qualifying immediate, unreduced and certain special early retirements. See MillionFed's FERS supplement earnings-test guide for rules that apply to people who actually qualify for the supplement. OPM: supplement eligibility clarification.

How do you apply after leaving federal service?

OPM now directs former employees seeking a postponed or deferred annuity to its Online Retirement Application (ORA) process. For postponed retirement, OPM's current guidance says to apply at least 31 days before the chosen commencement date and to follow its first-of-month date rules. For deferred retirement, OPM advises applying at least 60 days before the desired start. Read the current application instructions rather than relying on older articles that only mention paper form RI 92-19. OPM: application requirements.

Checklist before resigning or postponing your pension

  1. Confirm your exact MRA, service credit and retirement provision at separation with agency HR.
  2. Request a written pension estimate showing your high-3, age reduction and intended starting date.
  3. Ask HR to verify FEHB or USPS PSHB continuation, the five-year coverage rule, FEGLI eligibility and TCC deadlines.
  4. Budget for the years without annuity income or employer-subsidized health coverage.
  5. Confirm whether your situation involves an early-out, disability, special-category or other exception.
  6. Review your TSP strategy separately; a postponed FERS pension does not mean you must postpone every TSP decision.
