FERS Retirement Application Checklist: Timeline, ORA and Interim Pay

Applying for FERS retirement is more than choosing a final workday. This MillionFed checklist explains the current OPM application route, when to involve HR, how USPS coverage differs, and why interim pension payments may be lower than expected.

Quick answer: Start with your employing agency's benefits office well before retirement, review your service and insurance records, and follow its Online Retirement Application instructions. OPM recommends meeting with benefits staff at least 60 days before separation, and some agencies require more lead time.

Start with your agency—not a retirement calculator

Retirement eligibility and the paperwork to claim a pension are different things. If you are still employed, begin with your agency's human-resources or benefits office. OPM says your agency and payroll office must verify service and insurance records and send the retirement package to OPM. The OPM FERS retirement overview advises submitting ahead of separation and checking the agency's preferred deadline. An annuity estimate is not a completed application.

When should you start? A practical timeline

  • Six months or more out: request a retirement estimate; check your service history, deposits, military service and any court orders. Ask HR which records and documents are missing.
  • At least 60 days before your planned separation: contact your benefits office and follow its submission deadline. OPM's retirement application FAQ says agencies need a minimum of 60 days for their HR and payroll work, and some need longer. Its Quick Guide recommends meeting with benefits staff at least 60 days beforehand.
  • Before your last day: verify the service summary, election choices, signatures, personal contact information, and required supporting documents. Save copies of your records where permitted.
  • After separation: confirm the package was transmitted, monitor communications from OPM, and plan for possible interim payments before final adjudication.

These are planning milestones, not a guarantee of approval or a universal USPS deadline. Follow your own agency's current instructions.

How to apply: the Online Retirement Application (ORA)

OPM's current Planning and Applying guidance directs employees still working to contact their agency's HR office to begin the Online Retirement Application (ORA). The FERS immediate-retirement application is associated with SF 3107; ORA is the current submission platform described by OPM. Do not assume downloading an old PDF and mailing it yourself is the correct process for an active employee. Former employees seeking deferred or postponed retirement follow a different application route. For that distinction, read MillionFed's postponed versus deferred retirement guide.

Review the service summary and pension elections

Verify each period of federal employment, military service and any service deposit or redeposit. OPM warns that missing documentation can delay processing. Ask your benefits office to explain your certified service history and your high-3 estimate before signing. Review your spouse survivor election carefully: it can affect both the annuity and continuation of health coverage for eligible survivors. See what counts toward a FERS high-3 and FERS survivor benefit choices. If you have a divorce or other qualifying court order, identify it early; OPM lists court orders among cases that may take longer.

USPS employees: check PSHB, not just generic FEHB advice

For eligible Postal Service employees and annuitants, the Postal Service Health Benefits (PSHB) Program replaced the former FEHB enrollment route beginning in 2025. Ask USPS benefits staff to verify the rules applicable to your coverage and eligible family members; do not assume a generic federal employee checklist describes every USPS step. Also verify FEGLI eligibility and your survivor elections. OPM's retirement Quick Guide specifically tells retirees to review health and life insurance continuation before separation. Regular USPS letter carriers are generally covered by ordinary FERS retirement rules; special-category retirement provisions for certain law enforcement, firefighters or air traffic controllers should not be applied to letter carriers without an independent qualifying basis.

What documents should you gather?

  • Personnel and service records, including any periods at other federal agencies
  • Marriage certificates and applicable divorce decrees or court orders
  • Military service records and proof of any required deposit, where applicable
  • Information on prior workers' compensation claims or part-time service
  • Personal mailing address, telephone number, email and direct-deposit details
  • Records needed to verify health, life insurance and survivor elections

OPM's Application Tips warns that missing signatures and incorrect contact information can delay processing. Only supply sensitive documents through official channels designated by your agency or OPM.

How long does OPM take—and what is interim pay?

Do not budget on the assumption that a full pension starts immediately after your last paycheck. OPM's Retirement Quick Guide describes a typical multi-stage process of roughly three to five months in total, including agency/payroll processing, intake and OPM adjudication; individual cases can be faster or slower. OPM says interim payments are typically about 60%–80% of the estimated net annuity for most people, not 60%–80% of salary. They are temporary payments while the final claim is calculated—not a promise of an exact amount or start date. OPM's planning guidance also explains that insurance premium deductions may begin after finalization. Build a cash-flow cushion and confirm how your own deductions will be handled.

Why applications are delayed

OPM identifies missing records, incorrect forms, court orders, part-time or multi-agency service, certain workers' compensation claims, and special-category calculations as possible complications. Verify the dates and supporting evidence before submitting. If your application has not reached OPM, contact your former employing agency's HR office first; OPM's processing explanation distinguishes agency transmission from OPM adjudication. A status estimate is not an approval or a guarantee.

TSP and Social Security are separate applications

A FERS retirement application does not automatically start TSP withdrawals or Social Security retirement benefits. Those have separate rules and elections. If you are considering TSP withdrawals after leaving federal service, review the TSP rule of 55 and traditional versus Roth TSP withdrawals. For eligible immediate FERS retirees, the Special Retirement Supplement is a separate eligibility and computation issue; see MillionFed's FERS supplement calculation guide. Do not assume every retiree receives it.

Final checklist before submitting

Confirm your retirement eligibility and intended separation date with HR; compare your service record with your own documents; check high-3 and survivor elections; verify PSHB/FEHB and FEGLI continuation as applicable; resolve deposits and court-order questions; complete the current agency/ORA process; retain submission confirmation; and prepare for interim-pay uncertainty. These checks can reduce avoidable errors, but they cannot guarantee an OPM processing date.
